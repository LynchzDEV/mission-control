# Twenty Mission Control design explorations

## Objective

The user asks: “create like 20 set of you choice. i frame your idea too much. do whatever you would like with current information. go full creative codex!” Deliver 20 complete, materially different interactive UI design directions in a live gallery. This is design exploration, not production implementation. Twenty colour swaps of the same dashboard fail the objective.

User context: Terminals are 80% of use. Multiple simultaneous terminals and split/focus are wanted. Main is next, for workflow and cross-model usage, then Settings. Agent working chat is important but rarely opened: on demand, not a permanent sidebar. The user loved an animated ASCII atmosphere and wants playful, skilled motion, including dimensional Anime.js-like animation. Previous layouts were promising but generic cards, lavender panels, colour swaps, excessive text, and decorative art buried under a giant solid frame were rejected. They now grant creative freedom, including layout. Existing backend semantics remain a constraint for a future implementation, but all previews use sample data and no API calls.

## Twenty deliberate departures

Use these as art-direction seeds, with freedom in execution. Each page needs its own composition, not just the stated palette.

01 **Broadcast** — Live production desk, burnt orange, near-black and white, oversized condensed session lettering, terminal strips arranged like program/preview monitors. ASCII scan field with a short, purposeful tuning transition. No fake broadcast metrics.
02 **Phosphor** — Fluent terminal-native interface, phosphor green plus amber on black, command-driven hierarchy, minimal window framing. Dense useful output, quiet prompts, ASCII contour scanner. Avoid a fake CRT bezel or excessive flicker.
03 **Orbit** — Pearl white, ink blue and precise orange. A lightly dimensional deck of terminal surfaces with a clear main/peer relationship. An ASCII orbit or sphere occupies open space; focus straightens and enlarges the chosen surface. The object is ambience, never a workflow graph.
04 **Graphite** — Technical drawing on light grey paper. Exact black typography and vermilion action marks, shared central gutter, hairline rules that organise actual fields. Animated point-cloud strata. A confident light terminal design.
05 **Cobalt** — Saturated ultramarine as a structural surface, white terminals and small yellow action accents. Strong asymmetric 60/40 composition with controls along an edge rather than a standard top toolbar. Motion follows active-session changes.
06 **Carbon** — Almost frameless night workspace, sharp small type, silver and warm white. Two independent terminal regions, offset composition, bottom controls. A broad silver ASCII ribbon gives depth without hiding text. Very few buttons visible at rest.
07 **Letterpress** — A beautifully typeset operator's manual: warm paper, charcoal, rich blue; serif section identity with a true monospace terminal. Two generous folio columns and clear running-head controls. ASCII type impression or paper-feed motion. Avoid the cream/terracotta landing-page formula.
08 **Radar** — Deep marine blue and cyan, one dominant terminal with a compact live peer view that expands on selection. A restrained ASCII sweep/reveal. Spatial status without pretending engines are connected workflow nodes.
09 **Field Notes** — Pale yellow technical notebook, graphite and red-orange marks, terminals as differently proportioned working sheets. Margin actions and a slim session index. A quiet animated coordinate field. Handmade precision, not decorative doodles.
10 **Monochrome** — Bold, genuinely black-and-white composition; inverse terminal regions with strong typographic navigation and decisive whitespace. Animated ASCII typography or ordered dithering. No grey SaaS card kit.
11 **Ceramic** — White, jade, and dark slate; tactile precision-instrument controls and fine surface depth. Open, asymmetric terminals with a gently lifting focus transition. A soft, mathematically structured ASCII interference pattern.
12 **Departures** — Arrival/departure-board clarity: charcoal, warm yellow and restrained green, terminal sessions in horizontal tracks with activity visible at the edge. Crisp typographic rhythm and one mechanical reveal animation. Labels remain actual session and engine information.
13 **Glacier** — Luminous ice-blue and navy, architectural verticality, tall terminal columns and a narrow side index. Large ASCII terrain folds visible through negative space. Bright and expansive, with minimal surface decoration.
14 **Redshift** — Tomato red, black and white with bold but disciplined contrast. Offset terminals, compact underlined controls, a kinetic ASCII ribbon or Möbius form. Strong identity through composition and colour placement, not warning-coloured body text.
15 **Proof** — Crisp white and deep burgundy/ink, an editorial proof-reading desk with elegantly unequal columns and a firm baseline rhythm. Terminal output remains monospace. ASCII registration pattern as subtle motion. No marketing headline.
16 **Workbench** — Olive-grey industrial instrument, vivid amber action marks, terminals arranged as functional modules with compact rail controls. Session-switch motion borrows from physical trays; small dimensional details. No fake knobs or meaningless gauges.
17 **Tidal** — The most atmospheric option: petrol, pale coral and ivory. A wide, clearly visible animated ASCII fluid field; terminal planes compose into it while keeping reading surfaces opaque. Depth, calm movement, restrained controls. This should carry the original liked atmosphere strongly.
18 **Dot Matrix** — Paper white, black and punchy cherry red. Terminal output arranged as clean continuous-print strips; distinctive raster typography used sparingly. A moving dot-matrix band makes the identity. No fake OS title bars.
19 **Prism** — A genuinely spatial, playful experiment: clear session planes with small perspective, clean jewel/ice colours, a dimensional ASCII or wireframe construction in open space. On focus the selected plane becomes orthographic and readable. Animate depth meaningfully, respect reduced motion. No engine-network diagram.
20 **Play** — Surprising, optimistic and geeky: butter yellow, cool mint, black and a single hot accent; bold compact lettering, purposeful uneven terminal proportions, a session shelf with a playful mechanical response. ASCII glyph choreography. A usable tool with personality, not a toy dashboard.

At least six directions should be light; at least six should use distinct dimensional/spatial motion. Do not make all headings Avenir and all controls rounded pills. Available local/system fonts include Avenir Next, DIN Alternate, Menlo, Monaco, Helvetica Neue, Georgia, Baskerville, Courier New, Optima, and Trebuchet MS. ## Shared interaction floor

- Default shows at least two useful terminal sessions, with name, engine/model, a little believable command/output content, status, and command entry. Enough information to feel usable; no long paragraphs about the design itself. Text input is simulated and explicitly labelled once at a sensible location. Escape dynamic input before any HTML insertion.
- Selecting a session makes input ownership clear. Focus one terminal / restore the pair. Split may be side-by-side or stacked per direction. Preserve session transcript and draft through changes. Controls must be real preview interactions, not dead UI. A shared fixture/behavior runtime and accessible dialogs are appropriate across twenty layouts.
- Agent chat starts closed, opens for the selected session, contains typed activity / tool result and a working sample composer, preserves drafts and has explicit empty-send feedback. A no-agent sample state must be available. Chat can temporarily take space; closing it restores terminal space.
- Main and Usage reveal a matching compact view without losing the workspace. Plans are MANUAL steps (no automatic wiring/scheduling); show sensible task steps. Usage is heterogeneous: Claude cost/tokens/reset, GLM quota percentages, Codex availability. Never add percentages together or invent an all-provider total. An unavailable total is honest. Fixtures must be plainly sample data.
- Settings can be a small preview of existing role selection and appearance/motion controls. Never touch real preferences or credentials.
- At least one clearly observable nontrivial motion motif per direction: animated ASCII, a dimensional arrangement, or a purposeful spatial transition; combine these where coherent. An idle pulse on a dot alone does not meet the motion brief. Prominent atmospheric art needs visible room, not a giant opaque wrapper over it. No fake workflow links between engines.
- Pause/resume affects all ambient animation. Reduced motion renders a deliberate still composition. Stop background animation when the page is hidden; gallery should load only the active live page. Local `node_modules/animejs/dist/bundles/anime.umd.min.js` and its LICENSE may be copied once into this folder if useful. Native CSS/WAAPI is also appropriate. No new dependencies or remote scripts.
- Visible keyboard focus, correctly labelled inputs, readable contrast, usable at desktop 1512×909 and narrower 1000/768/375/320 without horizontal overflow or inaccessible controls. Collapse layout responsibly; do not merely hide overflow. Terminal output can scroll within its own pane.
